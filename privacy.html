Privacy Notice

What the Kinetix FSL app collects, why, who can see it, and your rights under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173). Please read it before you install the app.

Who we are

Kinetix FSL is a Filipino Sign Language learning app made by a student capstone team. It lets you learn signs, practise with your camera, and, if you make an account, join a small community.

Privacy questions and requests go to privacy@kinetixfsl.app.

What we collect

If you only use the app as a guest, your learning progress stays on your phone.

If you make an account we keep:

  • your name, email address and sign-in details (the password itself is held by Google, not by us)
  • your profile: display name, photo and banner if you add them, level, follower counts, whether you are online now or were active today (never an exact time), which communities you joined, and your notification choices
  • your learning progress: signs learned, points, streak and a log of activity
  • the devices you signed in on (a label and the last sign-in time)
  • what you post: posts, comments, votes, photos and videos you upload
  • your chats, notifications and reports you send or that are made about you
  • your year of birth as an age group (under 13, 13 to 17, or 18 and over), and which version of this notice you accepted
  • what you send from About: a rating, feedback or a bug report, with any screenshots or videos you attach, the app version, your phone’s maker and model, Android version and language

The camera is used on your phone to recognise your hand and body signs. The camera pictures are not saved or sent anywhere unless you record a clip yourself and choose to upload it.

Some things stay only on your phone and are never sent: your settings, your recent word searches, and the build and phone details (app version, phone model, Android version and memory) that Settings and About show and copy when you tap the build line. You paste those yourself, for example into a testing form.

Why we use it

We use your information to run your account, show your progress, keep the community working and safe (reports and moderation), send notifications you asked for, check for new versions of the app, and fix problems in the app. We do not sell your information and we do not show advertising.

Who can see it

  • Anyone, including people without an account, can see your public profile (name, photo, level, counts) and your posts and comments.
  • People signed in to the app can see who you follow and who follows you, and whether you are online now or were active today. For 13 to 17 year olds these stay hidden (see Children and young people).
  • People signed in to the app can see the members of a community.
  • Only you and the other person can read a chat. Administrators of the app can read chats and reports when they need to check a report or keep people safe.
  • Administrators can see learner progress and email addresses to run and improve the app.
  • The people who run the app’s servers (see the next section) handle the data for us.

Where it is stored

Your data is kept by services outside the Philippines: Google Firebase (sign-in, database, notifications, crash reports), Cloudflare (storage for photos and videos, and the server that handles uploads), Resend (emails such as sign-in codes) and GitHub (this website, the app download and the update check: when the app checks for a new version or downloads one, GitHub sees your phone’s IP address). By using the app you understand that your data is processed in other countries. We choose these services for their security measures, but we cannot control their own legal obligations.

How long we keep it

We keep your account data while your account exists. When you delete your account, the app removes your profile, progress, posts, comments, chats and uploaded files that it can find. Some records may be kept after that for safety and accountability, for example reports about content and records of moderation actions. Crash reports are kept by Google under its own rules.

Children and young people

  • If you are under 13 you cannot make an account. You can still learn as a guest. A parent or guardian can contact us at privacy@kinetixfsl.app.
  • If you are 13 to 17, a parent or guardian should know that you are joining, and should read this notice with you. Please be careful about what you share in public. The app hides your online status from other people and keeps your followers and following lists to yourself.
  • We ask only for your year of birth, and we keep it as an age group.

Your rights

Under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173) you may ask to see your data, correct it, delete it, object to how it is used, or withdraw your consent. You can delete your account in Settings. For anything else write to privacy@kinetixfsl.app. You also have the right to complain to the National Privacy Commission.

Security and problems

We protect the data with the security features of the services above and with limits on who can read what. No system is perfectly safe. If a serious breach affects your data, we will tell you and the National Privacy Commission within the time the law requires.

Videos used to train sign recognition

When you are 18 or older and you post a video, you can choose to let the Kinetix FSL team use it to train the sign recognition models of the app. This is optional and off unless you tick the box on that post. If you tick it, a SPED teacher may watch the video to check the sign, without your name, and a checked video may be used as training data. You can withdraw at any time by editing the post and unticking the box, by deleting the post, or by writing to privacy@kinetixfsl.app. A withdrawn video is left out of later training, but a model that was already trained cannot be changed.

Changes to this notice

This notice has a version number, shown at the top. When it changes in a way that matters, the new version is in the app (Settings) and on this website.

This website

This website has no analytics, advertising, forms or accounts. Your theme choice may be kept in your browser. The site is served by GitHub Pages, and the app download comes from GitHub Releases; both keep visitor IP addresses in their logs for security.
